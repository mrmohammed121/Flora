Bacterial Pathogen Crops: Tomato, Bell Pepper

Bacterial Spot

Xanthomonas perforans

A bacterial disease that thrives in warm, rainy weather, creating numerous small, water-soaked angular spots that tear into a distinctive shot-hole pattern.

🔬 Test Leaf Specimen in AI Studio

🔍 Diagnostic Symptoms & Visual Markers

  • Numerous small (1–3 mm), angular, dark brown to black water-soaked specks.
  • Brittle lesion centers that dry and fall out, creating a characteristic shot-hole effect.
  • Widespread leaf chlorosis and severe defoliation.
  • Raised, blistered scab spots with dark margins on immature fruit.

Visual Morphology Rule: Angular, greasy water-soaked specks bounded by small leaf veins. Does not respond to fungal fungicides.

🧬 Causes & Biology

Bacterium Xanthomonas species. Survives on crop residues and commercial seed lots; spread by rain-splash.

🚜 Cultural Prevention & Sanitation

  • Hot-water seed treatment at 50°C for 25 minutes.
  • Never work in fields when foliage is wet with morning dew.
  • Drip irrigation only; eliminate overhead sprinklers.

🌱 Certified Organic Remedies

Certified bacteriophages, copper octanoate soap, and microbial antagonists.

🧪 Chemical Protocols & FRAC Fungicides

Copper Hydroxide + Mancozeb tank mix, Acibenzolar-S-methyl (Actigard) plant defense SAR activator.

⚠️ Stewardship: Synthetic fungal QoI/DMI inhibitors are ineffective against bacterial pathogens.
