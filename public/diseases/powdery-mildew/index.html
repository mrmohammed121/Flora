Ectoparasitic Fungal Disease Crops: Cucumber, Squash, Melon, Zucchini

Powdery Mildew

Podosphaera xanthii

A widespread fungal disease that coats leaf surfaces in white, talcum-powder-like patches, leading to leaf chlorosis, premature drying, and reduced photosynthesis.

🔬 Test Leaf Specimen in AI Studio

🔍 Diagnostic Symptoms & Visual Markers

  • Circular, white, powdery talcum-like spots on upper and lower leaf surfaces.
  • White fungal patches expand and coalesce to cover the entire leaf blade.
  • Infected leaves turn yellow, then brown, and become dry, brittle, and paper-thin.
  • Severe defoliation exposes developing fruit to sunscald.

Visual Morphology Rule: Superficial white powdery talcum coating easily rubbed off with a finger. Does not require free standing water to infect.

🧬 Causes & Biology

Obligate biotrophic fungus Podosphaera xanthii. Conidia germinate under humid air without liquid water.

🚜 Cultural Prevention & Sanitation

  • Plant powdery mildew-resistant cucurbit varieties.
  • Space plants 60-90 cm apart for generous air circulation.
  • Trellis cucumber vines vertically to lift foliage off moist soil.

🌱 Certified Organic Remedies

Potassium bicarbonate (3-4 g/L) + horticultural oil (5 ml/L); diluted milk spray (40/60); sulfur.

🧪 Chemical Protocols & FRAC Fungicides

Myclobutanil (FRAC 3), Pyraclostrobin (FRAC 11). Rotate FRAC groups.

⚠️ Stewardship: Spray both upper and lower leaf surfaces thoroughly; powdery mildew often begins in shaded leaf undersides.
