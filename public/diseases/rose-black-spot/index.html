Ornamental Fungal Disease Crops: Rose (Rosa spp.)

Rose Black Spot

Diplocarpon rosae

The primary foliar disease of garden roses. Characterized by dark circular spots with distinct feathery, fibrillose edges, progressing to yellowing and severe defoliation.

🔬 Test Leaf Specimen in AI Studio

🔍 Diagnostic Symptoms & Visual Markers

  • Circular to irregular dark purple-brown to pitch-black spots on upper leaf surfaces.
  • Distinctive feathery, fibrillose, fringed spreading margins radiating outward.
  • Yellow chlorotic halos surrounding the black spots, causing premature leaflet drop.
  • Raised reddish-purple cankers on immature canes.

Visual Morphology Rule: Feathery, fibrillose margins with radiating fungal strands. Never exhibits concentric circular rings.

🧬 Causes & Biology

Ascomycete fungus Diplocarpon rosae. Conidia survive winter in fallen rose leaf litter.

🚜 Cultural Prevention & Sanitation

  • Water garden roses at the ground base; avoid wetting foliage.
  • Prune canes into an open vase shape to maximize airflow.
  • Mandatory sanitation: rake and burn all fallen rose leaves before winter.

🌱 Certified Organic Remedies

Potassium bicarbonate (3 g/L) + horticultural oil (5 ml/L), Bacillus amyloliquefaciens.

🧪 Chemical Protocols & FRAC Fungicides

Tebuconazole (FRAC 3), Trifloxystrobin + Tebuconazole (Nativo). Non-edible ornamental shrubs.

⚠️ Stewardship: CRITICAL PETAL SAFETY: Never spray fungicides directly onto open rose blooms or colored flower buds.
