📖 Agronomic Field Reference

Plant Pathology Reference Guide

Visual indicators, causal agents, and field symptoms for calibrated crop diseases

🎯

Early Blight

Alternaria solani
Fungal

Concentric "bulls-eye" target rings developing on older lower leaves. Surrounding tissue turns yellow (chlorotic halo) and defoliation progresses upward.

High-Risk Weather: Warm, humid spells (24–29°C) with alternating wet/dry cycles.
Primary Action: Apply Chlorothalonil or Azoxystrobin; mulch soil to halt spore splash.
🥀

Late Blight

Phytophthora infestans
Oomycete

Large, irregular water-soaked pale-green to dark brown lesions. Under high humidity, white velvet-like fungal down emerges on leaf undersides, collapsing foliage in days.

High-Risk Weather: Cool, foggy, saturated moisture (15–20°C, >90% RH).
Primary Action: Emergency spray of Metalaxyl-M + Mancozeb; quarantine infected vines immediately.
🍂

Bacterial Spot

Xanthomonas perforans
Bacterial

Small, angular, greasy water-soaked specks (1–3mm) turning dark purplish-brown. Lesion centers often dry up and fall out, giving a "shot-hole" appearance.

High-Risk Weather: Heavy rains, overhead irrigation, temperature 25–30°C.
Primary Action: Copper Hydroxide + Mancozeb synergy; avoid entering wet fields.
🌿

Healthy Foliage

Optimal Foliage (Healthy Specimen)
Optimal

Uniform rich green lamina with intact vascular venation. Normal photosynthetic leaf turgidity without necrotic spotting, chlorosis, or bacterial water-soaking.

Maintenance: Balanced N-P-K fertilization, drip irrigation, and routine scouting.
Preventative: Apply prophylactic Bacillus subtilis bio-spray every 14 days.
